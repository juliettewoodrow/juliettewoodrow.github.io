Lecture 1 · 25 minutes

Linear regression: a map inside GPT-2

GPT-2 turns the word "Chicago" into 768 numbers. Can a straight line turn those numbers back into a latitude and longitude? We learn linear regression on 1.4 million real New York taxi rides, then point it at the inside of a language model.

  • Predict with a weighted sum of features. The coefficients are the model.
  • Score a line by its mean squared error (ordinary least squares).
  • Learn the coefficients two ways: gradient descent, or solve directly.
  • Measure R² on rides the model never saw.
  • Fit two regressions on GPT-2's internal representations of 24,665 cities and test on 6,118 held-out cities.

Lecture 2 · 25 minutes

Inside a large language model

In 2022, researchers rewrote a fact inside GPT so it would say the Space Needle is in Paris. Where could a fact live inside a network? We open GPT-2 up, part by part, until the question has an answer.

  • From ten digit scores to 50,257 token scores: softmax gives every next token a probability.
  • Word embeddings are learned parameters, and similar words get similar parameters.
  • Attention lets a word read the words before it; learned weights decide how much from each.
  • GPT-2 running live in the browser, writing one token at a time.

Twenty-five minutes is not long. A few things I set aside, in one short deck of 23 slides:

Why not just ask GPT-2 where a city is? We did. It writes "about 1,000 miles from" and never gives a coordinate. What a model says is not the same as what it represents. slides 1 to 3
More cities through the probe. Tehran lands 70 km off. New York lands in the Atlantic. Cambridge, Massachusetts lands in Kansas: GPT-2 small does not know where MIT is. Then the debate: is a map in there, or is this parroting? slides 4 to 11
Reading R² honestly. R² = 0.75 does not mean 75% of trips were predicted correctly. Plus RMSE and MAE in minutes, and why the honest score is on held-out data. slides 12 to 17
The airport puzzle. Airport trips average 35 minutes, yet the airport feature gets a negative coefficient. Because the model already knows the distance, and given the distance, airport trips are faster. A coefficient is the effect of a feature holding the others fixed. slides 18 to 20
Bigger models, better maps. The same recipe on Llama 3.1 (8 billion parameters) gives a mean R² of 0.93 against 0.63 for GPT-2 small. Nobody asked either model to build a map. slides 21 to 23

Language Models Represent Space and Time
Wes Gurnee and Max Tegmark, MIT, 2023. The paper behind lecture 1. They fit ridge-regression probes on Llama-2's hidden states; the lecture's GPT-2 numbers are my own classroom reproduction with plain least squares.
Locating and Editing Factual Associations in GPT
Kevin Meng, David Bau, Alex Andonian and Yonatan Belinkov, NeurIPS 2022. The Space Needle experiment that opens lecture 2.
Attention Is All You Need
Vaswani et al., 2017. The transformer, which GPT-2 is one of.
An Introduction to Statistical Learning
James, Witten, Hastie, Tibshirani and Taylor. Chapter 3 is the textbook treatment of linear regression, free online.
3Blue1Brown, Neural networks
Grant Sanderson's visual series. The chapters on transformers and attention pair well with lecture 2.
NYC Taxi Trip Duration
The taxi data: 1,458,644 yellow-cab trips, January to June 2016, from the NYC Taxi and Limousine Commission via Kaggle. Trips under a minute or over two hours were removed; 1,442,754 remain, split 80/20 at random before any fitting.
